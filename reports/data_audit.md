## 7. Cleaning rules and targets

All rules live in `configs/default.yaml` (`clean:`), are applied by one function (`fremtpl2_pricing.clean.clean`) to the full dataset before splitting, and treat every policy identically regardless of set. The decisions on the judgement calls below were taken by the human on 2026-11-07 (section 8).

**R0 identifiers** (`duplicate_idpol: error`): 0 duplicated IDpol measured, so nothing is removed; the rule makes the pipeline fail if a future data version contains duplicates. IDpol is normalised to int64 (freq dtype int64, sev dtype int64; a non-integral value is an error). Join cardinality: many_to_one (sev -> freq), freq IDpol unique: True, 24,950 distinct IDpol in sev.

**R1 exposure**: cap at 1: 1,224 policies (0.18%) have Exposure > 1 (maximum 2.01), carrying 54 claims; capping removes 139.3 policy-years (0.039% of exposure). Assumption (not testable from the data): Exposure above 1 year is a recording artefact; the cap keeps the policy and its claims. Exposure that is not finite or <= 0 is quarantined: 0 policies (0 severity rows). Exposure stays out of the rating-factor list; it is only an offset / weight.

**R2 minimum exposure: none (all positive exposures kept, no floor)**. Measured on recorded ClaimNb: 3,105 policies (0.46%) have Exposure <= 0.00274, with 50 claims and an annualised frequency of 5.88 against 0.101 for the portfolio. Policies up to 0.05 years total 49,342 rows, 1,455 claims (4.03% of claims) and 0.381% of exposure. A floor would invent exposure and any cut-off would be chosen after looking at outcomes; short-record exclusions are pre-registered sensitivities (section 8). Frequencies on matched rows are in section 12.

**R3 ClaimNb: no clipping**. Recorded ClaimNb above 4: 9 policies (values [5, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 11, 11, 16], exposures [1.0, 0.08, 0.33, 0.41, 0.08, 1.0, 0.08, 0.07, 0.33]). Counts are re-audited with N_i in section 11.

**R4 severity rows**: amounts must be finite and > 0, otherwise quarantined: 0 rows quarantined. Rows whose IDpol is absent from freq (drop): 195 rows (6 policies), amount 788,714.18 (1.30% of total cost). They have no rating factors, exposure or split side, so they are excluded from modelling and written to `results/orphan_ledger.csv` (IDpol, ClaimAmount); they stay in the reconciliation (section 9).

**R5 ClaimNb versus severity rows**: claims_without_sev = flag (9,116 policies, 9,657 claims, 26.75% of claims), sev_without_claims = flag (0 policies), count_mismatch = flag (1 policy). Policies are kept and flagged in `sev_status`. "No severity row" does not prove an unpaid, closed-without-payment or zero-cost claim; the target is called the recorded matched claim cost, not an estimate of ultimate cost.

## 8. Post-audit, pre-model amendments (human decision 2026-11-07)

Decided after this audit and before any model is run. They are benchmark design choices, not proof that the underlying records are correct. The split assignment registered earlier stays frozen.

1. **Evaluation split.** The grouped split (group = tuple of the rating factors, stratified on claim presence ClaimNb > 0) stays the primary and is not re-assigned; the random split is a sensitivity. It is a rating-profile holdout, not customer-level leakage prevention: identical covariates do not establish the same policyholder, and one customer with changed attributes can straddle the split. Wüthrich and Merz flag likely renewal-split records; this is cited only as a convention. Assignments are never re-searched; `fremtpl2 split` recomputes the assignment in memory and fails if it differs from the stored `data/processed/split.parquet`, which it never overwrites.
2. **Large losses.** Per-claim cap 100,000 applied before aggregation; raw amounts and excess are kept separately. Primary comparison on the limited cost without reloading. Optional gross illustration: a training-only flat annual loading per exposure-year (`clean.excess_loading.mode: flat_per_exposure_year`), sum of train excess / sum of train exposure, orphans excluded, never estimated on the test set (value in `results/targets_summary.json`). The scikit-learn Tweedie tutorial caps at 200,000 per policy after aggregation, not per claim, so it is not equivalent to a per-claim cap; cited only as a convention.
3. **Claims without severity.** Option (b): frequency target N_i = matched severity rows (`frequency_count: sev_rows`), so frequency x severity and Tweedie share one observed-cost target. ClaimNb_raw and a discrepancy flag are kept for the audit only. Recorded-ClaimNb frequency (option a) is the sensitivity `frequency_recorded_claim_nb`. Amounts must be finite and > 0, else quarantined (0 found).
4. **ClaimNb clipping** is disabled in the primary rules. High counts are re-audited with N_i (section 11). Whole-policy exclusion of N_i > 5 (frequency, severity and Tweedie alike) is the sensitivity `exclude_high_count`.
5. **Exposure.** All finite exposures > 0 are kept (no floor), e <= 0 is quarantined, the cap at 1.0 year is unchanged, and exposure is never a rating factor (offset / weight only). Short-record exclusion and raw (uncapped) exposure are sensitivities.
6. **Orphan severity rows** are excluded from modelling, written to `results/orphan_ledger.csv` and kept in the reconciliation (section 9); cap and excess impacts use matched claims only.

Pre-registered sensitivities (declared in `clean.sensitivities`, selectable by name via `clean.clean_sensitivity`; no model has been run on them): `exclude_high_count`, `frequency_recorded_claim_nb`, `cap_50000`, `cap_200000`, `cap_uncapped`, `raw_exposure`, `exclude_short_records_1day`, `exclude_short_records_0_05`.
