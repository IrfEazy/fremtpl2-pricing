## 7. Cleaning rules and justification

All rules live in `configs/default.yaml` (`clean:`), are applied by one function (`fremtpl2_pricing.clean.clean`) to the full dataset before splitting, and treat every policy identically regardless of set.

**R0 duplicate IDpol** (`error`): 0 duplicates measured, so nothing is removed; the rule makes the pipeline fail if a future data version contains duplicates.

**R1 exposure cap at 1**: 1,224 policies (0.18%) have Exposure > 1 (maximum 2.01), carrying 54 claims; capping removes 139.3 policy-years (0.039% of exposure). Claims per policy in these rows: 0.0441, portfolio: 0.0532. Assumption (not testable from the data): Exposure above 1 year is a recording artefact and not additional risk time; the cap keeps the policy and its claims. Dropping the rows instead would discard 54 claims.

**R2 minimum exposure: none (all small exposures kept)**. Measured: 3,105 policies (0.46%) have Exposure <= 0.00274, with 50 claims and an annualised frequency of 5.88 against 0.101 for the portfolio (ratio 58.4). Policies up to 0.05 years total 49,342 rows, 1,455 claims (4.03% of claims) and 0.381% of exposure. The share of policies with a claim across the thresholds ranges from 1.51% to 2.82%, against 5.02% for the portfolio. Nothing is excluded: any cut-off would be chosen after looking at outcomes. The exposure offset and later sensitivity checks must handle these rows. **For human review**: decide whether to set `clean.exposure_min` (candidate values are the thresholds in `audit.exposure_small_thresholds`) and compare.

**R3 ClaimNb cap at 4**: 9 policies with ClaimNb > 4 (values [5, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 11, 11, 16], exposures [1.0, 0.08, 0.33, 0.41, 0.08, 1.0, 0.08, 0.07, 0.33]), i.e. 46 claims above the cap (0.127% of claims, 0.001% of exposure). Their claims per policy-year are 24.26 against 0.101 for the portfolio. Capping keeps the policy and its exposure; policies at or below 4 are untouched. The threshold 4 is a judgement call. **For human review.**

Severity rows of these policies: 71 rows with amount 167,225.70. The ClaimNb cap changes only the frequency count; severity rows are never capped or counted differently because of it (they are kept), so on these policies ClaimNb after the cap and the number of severity rows can differ (see targets below).

**R4 severity rows without a freq policy (drop)**: 195 rows, amount 788,714.18 (1.30% of total cost). They have no rating factors, exposure or split side, so they cannot be modelled or assigned to train/test; they are dropped and counted here. **For human review**: whether to keep them out of all cost totals.

**R5 ClaimNb versus severity rows**: claims_without_sev = flag (9,116 policies, 9,657 claims, 26.75% of claims), sev_without_claims = flag (0 policies), count_mismatch = flag (1 policy). Policies are kept (flagged in `sev_status`) because dropping them would delete 26.75% of observed claims from the frequency model. Assumption: claims without severity rows were closed without payment or not yet valued; the data cannot confirm this. **For human review.**

### Target definitions (config `clean.targets`; for human review)

- Severity sample: policies with at least one severity row (24,944 policies, 26,444 rows). Severity weight (`sev_rows`): the number of severity rows; total weight 26,444.
- Frequency count (`claim_nb`): ClaimNb as recorded, after the ClaimNb cap; total 36,056 against 36,056 ClaimNb after cleaning.
- Pure-premium observed cost: the sum of the capped severity amounts of the policy, total 49,302,196.05; claims without severity rows contribute zero cost (`zero_cost`; sample 678,013 policies).

Consequence: 9,651 claims (26.77% of ClaimNb after cleaning) have no severity row. A frequency x severity pure premium counts them at the average severity (cost per severity row 1,864.40), whereas a Tweedie / pure-premium target treats them as zero cost (`pure_premium_claims_without_sev: zero_cost`). The two routes are therefore not on the same target unless the frequency count is restricted to claims with severity rows. If those claims were valued at the average cost per severity row, the cost missing from the Tweedie target would be 17,993,325.29, 26.74% of the total (this is an illustration under that assumption, not a measured loss). Options, **not chosen here: for human review**: (a) frequency on ClaimNb as recorded (`frequency_count: claim_nb`, configured; keeps all claims, inconsistent with Tweedie); (b) frequency on the count of severity rows (`frequency_count: sev_rows`, alternative; consistent with Tweedie, drops the claims without severity rows from the frequency target). The configured value (`claim_nb`) is kept only so downstream work has a defined target.

**R6 per-claim cap at 100,000**: 41 claims exceed it (0.154% of claims) and the capped excess is 10,607,020.45 (17.71% of total cost). The largest single claim is 6.71% of total cost and the ten largest together 15.64%. The cap lies between the 99.5% quantile (34,387) and the 99.9% quantile (162,784) of the raw amounts; the table in section 3 shows how much cost moves for other caps (the table counts all raw severity rows, including the R4 rows without a policy, which are removed before capping). The choice is a judgement call between stability and bias: capped targets understate pure premium by the removed share, which must be reported separately. Uncapped amounts are kept in `ClaimAmount_uncapped`. **For human review.**
